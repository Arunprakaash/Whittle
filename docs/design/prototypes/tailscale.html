Your computer, from anywhere, with Tailscale

Today, Use from your computer works only on the same Wi-Fi. With Tailscale, your computer reaches the phone from any network: mobile data, a café, another city. Tailscale runs inside Whittle's Linux, like the SSH server, so there's no extra app and no VPN on the phone. It adds one row and one sign-in to the Your computer section. Everything else is the section you already have.

  • Optional, and yoursOff until you set it up. You sign in with your own Tailscale account (free for personal use) in the browser; Whittle never sees your password.
  • Wi-Fi or TailscaleThe switch works whenever either is there. Away from Wi-Fi it carries on over Tailscale, instead of switching off.
  • Keys still decideBeing on your tailnet lets a device knock; only computers you added can sign in.
  • Says what it opensDevices on your tailnet can also open servers Whittle runs, like a preview on port 5173. The footer says so.

Setting it up

Set up → installs Tailscale in Linux (about 70 MB, once) → Sign in opens Tailscale's page in your browser → the row turns Connected by itself.

Checked on the emulator

Runs inside LinuxTailscale 1.104.2 installed from Tailscale's own repository in 23 s and ran under proot in userspace mode: no VPN, no root, no separate app.
Sign-in by linktailscale up printed a login link; signing in there joined the phone to the tailnet as whittle-emulator.
SSH from the MacKey sign-in to 100.119.93.43 in 0.29 s over a direct connection, a real terminal, and a dev server through ssh -L.
No extra listenerTailscale hands incoming connections to the phone's loopback, where SSH already listens. Whittle only keeps SSH on while Tailscale is connected.

Still to choose

Phone's name on the tailnetProposed: "whittle-" plus the phone's model, like whittle-pixel-9. You can rename it in Tailscale's admin console.
Remove TailscaleSign out keeps it installed; "Remove Tailscale too" in Sign out's dialog takes it out. Set up but never signed in, tapping the row offers Remove.
Tailscale's markTailscale's own one-colour mark, tinted by the theme like Health Connect's. The prototype uses a stand-in.

Wording follows the Settings rule: a row's second line says its state, and only when there is one.
